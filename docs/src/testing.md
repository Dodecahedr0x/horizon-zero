# Testing

Two CI jobs live in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. Both install Anza `v4.2.1` and Node 24, whose default type stripping lets the tests and scripts import `app/src/codec.ts` directly.

## Combat, wasm, and LiteSVM

Job `litesvm` uses Rust `1.97.1`.

1. `cargo test -p combat`, including the golden trace.
   `cargo test -p horizon-zero` runs the program's host tests: account sizes, status bytes, `Player::free`, the fee arithmetic, and a round trip of the battle layout through `write_battle`, Borsh, and the hand-written reader `settle_match` uses.
2. `cargo-build-sbf` for `programs/mock-vrf`.
3. `cargo-build-sbf` for `programs/horizon-zero` with `--features inline-mock-vrf,inline-mock-magic`.
4. `cargo test -p horizon-litesvm-tests -- --test-threads=1`.
5. `wasm-pack` builds of `crates/combat` (`--features wasm`) into `app/src/combat-node` and `app/src/combat-wasm`.
6. `pnpm --filter app test` and `pnpm --filter asset-studio test`. The app command runs the combat golden replay, the codec checks (`app/src/codec.test.mjs`), the SOL amount parser and error reading (`app/src/money.test.mjs`), and `app/local-ledger.test.mjs`.

LiteSVM loads `horizon_zero.so` at `Hm19JfKaKCRphRwF7DbxhJWmLpHgMG1SnEnKGRyN9Z7c` and `mock_vrf.so` at `Vrf1RNUjXmQGjmQrQLvJHs9SNkvDJEsRVFPkfSQUwGz`. The suite creates its own oracle-queue account, owned by the VRF program id, with 256 zero bytes. That is enough for the ownership check. It is not a MagicBlock queue.

The fixture loads the program with `add_program`, which deploys it through the upgradeable loader with no upgrade authority, rewrites its ProgramData so the admin is the upgrade authority, and calls `initialize(500, mAGicPQYBMvcYveUZA5F5UNNwyHvfYh5xkLS2Fr1mev)` with the program and ProgramData accounts, then `create_battleground` for the three catalog maps: Outpost `(0, budget 8, stake 10_000_000, 8×6)`, Garrison `(1, budget 16, stake 50_000_000, 10×8)`, and Citadel `(2, budget 32, stake 100_000_000, 12×10)`. Each account is 576 bytes and stores `zone_depth` 2. It then registers the admin with a 1 SOL deposit, so the admin's Player sponsors the shared behaviors. The army and stake helpers register any other wallet on first use with a 0.2 SOL deposit, and `cover_stake` tops a Player up with a plain transfer when its free lamports cannot fund one stake plus a battle's rent.

`programs/mock-vrf` accepts a request and fulfills it on a second instruction, because a callback into `horizon-zero` while `lock_army` is still on the stack is rejected.

| first byte | mock behavior |
| --- | --- |
| 10 or 11 | request accepted, no callback |
| 12 | parse the request and invoke the callback immediately |
| anything else | return success and do nothing |

Instruction 12's callback data is the 8-byte `seed_match` discriminator, then the caller seed, then the request args. The caller seed is therefore the `randomness` argument. Tests assert the stored battle seed equals `SHA-256(battle pubkey)`.

`inline-mock-vrf` makes `request_vrf` invoke the VRF program with a plain `invoke_signed` on the request's own accounts; the mock accepts it without a callback. The tests append a separate instruction 12 after `lock_army` in the same transaction, which runs `seed_match`. That feature is absent from the localnet build.

`inline-mock-magic` stands in for the magic program, which LiteSVM does not ship. Ephemeral accounts become ordinary PDAs funded from the sponsoring Player at the Solana rent-exempt minimum (see [Ephemeral accounts](accounts.md#ephemeral-accounts)), and the vault and magic program accounts are not checked; the tests pass the real addresses anyway. Rent amounts in the tests are therefore Solana rent, not `(len + 60) * 32`.

The match test follows one battle through every step: after the lock each Player has moved one stake from `budget` to `locked` and counts one fight, neither Player's lamports moved except the locker's battle rent, and the 2155-byte battle carries `fee_bps` 500; after the callback the battle is Seeded with the seed stored, and replaying the callback fails with `AlreadyResolved`. A fresh wallet then settles: the logged outcome equals the host simulation, both armies are Idle, the battle is closed and its rent is back on the locker's Player, and on a win the loser's Player has lost the stake, the winner's has gained `stake - fee`, the winner's budget holds `pot - fee`, and Config holds the fee in lamports and in `fees`. A second, draw-only battle moves no lamports and returns each stake to its budget. It also asserts that `seed_match` stays under 100 000 CU. `each_outcome_moves_exact_lamports_and_counters` pins a side-A win, a side-B win, and a draw with asymmetric squads checked against the host simulation, and asserts both Players' lamports and counters and Config's lamports and `fees` exactly; on the side-B win the locker's Player gets both the battle rent and the payout. `a_second_settle_fails` refuses a settlement before the VRF callback (`NotSeeded`), then one with the two Players swapped (`BadPda`), settles once, and then gets `AlreadyResolved` with every counter unchanged. `an_unseeded_battle_cancels_after_the_wait_and_refunds_both_stakes` opens a battle with no VRF callback at a set clock time: `cancel_match` fails with `TooEarly` at once and 599 seconds later, and with `BadPda` when the Players are swapped; at 600 seconds a fresh wallet cancels it, both Players' counters and lamports are back to their values before the lock, both armies are Idle and the battle is closed. A late callback then fails, `settle_match` and a second `cancel_match` fail with `AlreadyResolved`, and both armies fight again. `a_seeded_battle_cannot_be_cancelled` gets `AlreadySeeded` long after the wait, then settles the battle.

`initialize_needs_a_validator_identity` clears Config and the identity PDA, then runs `initialize` again: an all-zeros `validator` fails with `BadValidator`, and the local validator's identity succeeds and is stored at offset 50. `only_the_upgrade_authority_initializes_and_a_funded_identity_does_not_block_it`: a stranger fails with `NotUpgradeAuthority`, so does a stranger passing another program's ProgramData that names it, and so does the admin once the program has no upgrade authority; an identity address holding 1 000 lamports is topped up to rent and assigned, and one holding more than rent keeps its lamports. Four tests cover the Player. `fee_is_capped_and_only_the_admin_sets_it`: `set_fee` above 1000 fails with `FeeTooHigh` and from a stranger with `NotAdmin`. `register_then_fund_budget_moves_free_lamports_only`: the deposit lands above the Player's rent, `fund_budget` moves no lamports, more than the free balance fails with `BalanceShort`, and a top-up transfer makes it fundable again. `army_rent_comes_from_the_player_and_returns_on_close`: the behavior and army rent leave the Player, not the wallet, and closing the army refunds its rent to the Player. `rent_cannot_dip_into_the_budget`: with every free lamport in the budget, `create_behavior` fails with `RentShort`.

Four tests cover money leaving the rollup. Under `inline-mock-magic`, `withdraw` and `claim_fees` run only their checks and schedule nothing, so the tests call `release_outbox` themselves, as the post-undelegate action would. `withdraw_takes_free_then_winnings_then_budget_and_release_pays_the_owner`: after a segregated win, `fund_outbox` of 0 fails with `NothingToWithdraw`, a stranger's signature on the owner's accounts with `ConstraintSeeds`, `withdraw` of an empty Outbox with `NothingToWithdraw`, and a stranger's `withdraw` on the owner's Outbox with `ConstraintSeeds`. A withdrawal of every free lamport plus 10 000 000 leaves the budget alone and takes the rest from `winnings`; the Player loses exactly that, and the Outbox holds its rent plus it. Both instructions log (`HZ_OUTBOX_FUND`, `HZ_WITHDRAW`). Swapping the funded Outbox's account owner for the system program makes `withdraw` fail with `BadPda`. A second `fund_outbox` fails with `OutboxBusy`. A stranger's release to itself fails with `ConstraintAddress`, a stranger's release to the owner pays the owner exactly and leaves the Outbox its rent, and a repeat moves and logs nothing. A second withdrawal takes the rest of the winnings, then 15 000 000 of the budget; one lamport more than the remaining budget fails with `BalanceShort`, and withdrawing exactly the budget leaves the Player at its rent. `funding_an_outbox_keeps_the_players_rent_and_counters` sets a Player one lamport short of its rent plus budget: funding the whole budget, or a single lamport, fails with `BalanceShort`, and after a one-lamport top-up the whole budget leaves and the Player is left at its rent. `withdraw_never_touches_locked_stakes`: with one stake locked in a seeded battle, one lamport more than free plus budget fails with `BalanceShort`, exactly that leaves the Player at rent plus the stake, and the battle still settles and returns the stake to the budget. `only_the_admin_claims_fees_and_release_pays_the_admin`: after a won battle, a stranger's `init_treasury_outbox`, `fund_treasury_outbox` and `claim_fees` fail with `NotAdmin`, and `claim_fees` before funding fails with `NothingToWithdraw`. With Config one lamport short of its rent plus the fees, funding fails with `BalanceShort`. The admin's claim zeroes `config.fees`, leaves Config at its rent, and puts the fee in the treasury Outbox; a second funding fails with `OutboxBusy`; the release pays the admin the fee; with no fees left, funding fails with `NothingToWithdraw`.

The five delegate instructions are compiled out under `inline-mock-magic`, so the suite cannot run them. `solana_admin_checks_read_config_by_hand` covers the hand-read of Config they share with `create_battleground` and `init_treasury_outbox`, through those two. Config is 83 bytes with the pinned validator at offset 50. `init_treasury_outbox` given the admin's Player as Config fails with `AccountDiscriminatorMismatch`. Config's bytes copied to another address, still owned by this program, fail both instructions with `BadPda` (the address check). The real Config with the system program as owner fails both with `BadPda` (the owner check). The real Config owned by the delegation program, as a delegated Config's Solana copy is: a stranger's call to either fails with `NotAdmin`, and the admin's `create_battleground` and `init_treasury_outbox` both succeed.

`a_rootborn_army_stores_its_own_catalog_rows` creates a race `1` army in Outpost: Rootborn kind `3` fails with `BadUnit`, race `3` with `BadRace`, three Thornlings (cost 12) go over the budget, and two Thornlings are stored with race byte `1` and the Thornling's catalog cost and hp. A Clawling and a Spineshot then make a race `2` army, stored with race byte `2` and the Clawling's cast damage; the unknown race is `3`.

`close_army_needs_the_owner_and_an_idle_army` checks that a stranger gets `NotOwner`, that a queued army gets `BadStatus`, that after `unlock_army` the owner closes the army, logs `HZ_ARMY_CLOSE`, and the owner's Player gets the rent back, and that the same index then creates a new army at the same address. `app/local-ledger.test.mjs` checks the ledger's delete route the same way (refused while Fighting, then gone, then 404) and the per-player match list.

`full_armies_resolve_on_every_battleground` plays full-budget armies on all three battlegrounds, 10 units a side on Citadel, through the mock VRF and `settle_match`, prints what `settle_match` consumed, and checks that settling closed the battle; [Callback compute](matchmaking.md#callback-compute) lists the numbers. The suite also checks duplicate behaviors (`AlreadyExists`), hash mismatch, budget, unknown behavior, wrong owner, a position outside the zone, a repeated tile, winner payment, draw refund, and that a player does not match their own queued army or a queued army in another battleground.

The suite's `queue` helper passes the battle read-only, as a lock that only queues must in the rollup (see [`lock_army`](accounts.md#lock_army)); locks that open a match pass it writable. Five tests cover stakes. `locking_needs_a_budget_that_covers_the_stake_and_unlock_dequeues`: locking with an empty budget, then with a budget one lamport short, fails with `BudgetTooLow`; a covered lock queues without moving lamports or counters, and unlock leaves the queue. `one_player_backs_many_armies_until_it_runs_dry`: one owner queues three armies on a budget of two stakes; two lockers each meet a different one of them while a stake at a time moves from `budget` to `locked`; the third locker, given the owner's Player, evicts the last army (`HZ_EVICT`, Idle) and queues, and the owner's next lock fails with `BudgetTooLow`. `an_army_whose_owner_cannot_cover_a_stake_is_evicted` drains an owner's budget with one match, then shows that a lock without the owner's Player, or with a forged program-owned Player copy away from the PDA, leaves the stranded army queued, and that a lock with the real Player evicts it. `a_player_does_not_match_their_own_army_or_another_battleground` offers the player's own queued army and Player to its next lock, and the same armies to a lock on another battleground; both queue instead of matching. `segregated_wins_stay_out_of_the_budget_and_draws_refund_it`: a stranger cannot set another wallet's segregation (`ConstraintSeeds`); with it on, a win the host simulation predicts pays `pot - fee` into `winnings`, which a lock cannot stake; a draw still returns the stake to the budget; switching segregation off moves nothing. Combat tests cover the blocked-tile rules, including a one-column choke that stays open and a scripted tile a unit does not step on. The host comparison that finds which battleground an army stored is a byte slice of account data. The program itself does not compare `AccountInfo` keys with `[u8; 32] ==`.

## Local stack

Job `localnet` caches the cargo builds of the workspace and of `indexer/` (`Swatinem/rust-cache`), installs the indexer's toolchain from `indexer/rust-toolchain.toml` and builds the indexer in `indexer/`, so the stack's `cargo run` starts it at once. It then installs `@magicblock-labs/ephemeral-validator@0.14.10` globally and runs `pnpm localnet:check`, which is `scripts/local-stack.mjs --check`. The same script without `--check` is `pnpm localnet`, the local cluster for development: [Networks and deployment](deployment.md#local-stack) describes what it starts and the bootstrap it runs. It builds `programs/horizon-zero` with default features, so neither `inline-mock-vrf` nor `inline-mock-magic` is on: the rollup runs the real ephemeral-account, delegation, commit and VRF paths. Every start resets the base ledger, the rollup's storage and the indexer database.

With `--check`, `app/scripts/localnet-assert.mjs` then runs against Solana (`8899`), the rollup (`7799`), the ephemeral test queue, the bootstrap admin `target/localnet/admin.json` and the stack's indexer. It imports the instruction builders and decoders from `app/src/codec.ts`. It sends every transaction the way the game does: a blockhash from the connection it sends to, then polling `isBlockhashValid` and `getSignatureStatuses` with `searchTransactionHistory`, and `getTransaction` for the logs. Any failed check stops it with a non-zero exit:

1. The rollup returns its identity, Config exists, the identity is the validator Config pins, and the keypair is Config's admin. With `--probe`, a fresh blockhash must be valid on both connections, and still valid 10 seconds later.
2. Two new wallets each send `register_player` (deposit 0), `delegate_player` and `delegate_outbox` in one Solana transaction, then wait until the rollup holds the Player and the Outbox as this program's.
3. Each tops its Player up with 0.05 SOL through `lamportsDelegatedTransferIx`; the rollup must credit exactly 0.05 SOL. Each then sends `fund_budget` of 0.02 SOL in the rollup.
4. Each creates a standard behavior (March, Charge) and a full Outpost army of race 0 on it, in the rollup. The behavior and the army are ephemeral: owned by the program, 0 lamports.
5. The first wallet's `lock_army` queues, with the battle read-only. The second offers that army as its candidate, with the battle writable, and opens the match: the battle exists as an ephemeral account and the locker's Player paid its rent. The rollup's oracle seeds it within 60 seconds.
6. The script replays the battle with `simulate_json` from `app/src/combat-node` on the stored grid, snapshots and seed, then sends `settle_match`. The outcome in `HZ_MATCH_RESOLVED` must match the replay, and the battle must be closed. On a win, the fee is `2 × stake × fee_bps / 10 000` (1 000 000 lamports at 500 bps), the winner's Player gained `stake − fee` lamports and its budget grew by as much, the loser's Player lost the stake and its budget too, and Config's lamports and `config.fees` grew by the fee. On a draw, nothing moved and both budgets are back at 0.02 SOL. Both Players have nothing locked or fighting.
7. The first wallet closes its army (`close_army`): the army is gone and its Player got the rent back.
8. With `--probe` only: `getProgramAccounts` on the rollup lists an ephemeral army and a delegated Player; `getMultipleAccounts` returns them; `logsSubscribe` and `programSubscribe` on the rollup's WebSocket have sent notifications; `getTransaction`, `getSignatureStatuses` with history and `getBlockTime` are recorded.
9. The winner (the first wallet on a draw) sends `fund_outbox` of 0.01 SOL and `withdraw` in one rollup transaction. Within 60 seconds the Outbox is the program's again on Solana with `amount` 0. The base transaction that logged `HZ_RELEASE` must be signed by the rollup's identity and also contain `ProcessUndelegation`: the post-undelegate action paid it, not a fallback. The wallet rose by at least 0.01 SOL, and the Player lost exactly 0.01 SOL in the rollup.
10. If there were fees, the admin sends `fund_treasury_outbox` and `claim_fees`; the same checks apply to the treasury Outbox, the admin rose by at least the fees, and `config.fees` is 0.
11. The wallet sends `delegate_outbox` and the admin `delegate_treasury_outbox`; the rollup holds both again.
12. The indexer's `/matches/<battle>` shows the battle Resolved with the same outcome, fee and `feeBps`, and with both times set.

Lines starting with `FEE` record what each step cost a wallet. `pnpm localnet:check --probe` (or `node scripts/local-stack.mjs --check --probe`) also prints, as `PROBE` lines, what the rollup's RPC answered and the account shapes; it is off by default because it adds about 40 seconds of waits, and CI runs without it. The evidence from the first run is in `docs/plans/2026-10-14-outbox-spike-result.md`. The script exits, and the stack stops, after the last check.

## Compute benchmark

`cu_benchmark` in `tests/litesvm-suite/tests/game.rs` is ignored by default. It plays a fixed, seeded set of battles through `settle_match`: 8 per battleground, each between two random budget-legal armies, each army on one random standard behavior, with a random VRF seed. The settlement runs in its own transaction with the whole 1 400 000 CU, and the VM runs on mainnet's runtime features. It prints the mean and maximum per battleground and the total, and writes every battle's compute, rounds and outcome to `target/cu-bench.json`; it also asserts each on-chain outcome equals the host simulation. Comparing outcomes and rounds between two runs shows that an optimization changed no battle.

```sh
cargo-build-sbf --manifest-path programs/horizon-zero/Cargo.toml -- --features inline-mock-vrf,inline-mock-magic
cargo test -p horizon-litesvm-tests cu_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture
```

Built with `--features "inline-mock-vrf inline-mock-magic cu-profile"` (the program feature turns on `combat/cu-profile`), the engine also reads the remaining compute around each phase and logs per-phase totals at the end of a battle (`crates/combat/src/profile.rs`); the benchmark's VM enables the `sol_remaining_compute_units` syscall and a 10 000 000 CU limit for that build, and prints each phase's net cost (minus about 100 CU per probe), share and calls: rubble, setup, movement (with its target choice, path lookups and wave steps), actions (with their target choice), expiry, and the program's work before the simulation. Without the feature the probes compile to nothing.

| step | total for the 24 battles | change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before | 14 233 385 CU | |
| tighter path-search loops | 12 970 118 | -8.9% |
| one walking-distance field per goal | 10 588 549 | -25.6% |
| skip the lookup next to the goal | 10 003 032 | -29.7% |
| resumable per-goal waves | 8 920 606 | -37.3% |
| occupancy bit masks | 8 198 466 | -42.4% |
| double-buffered waves | 7 652 482 | -46.2% |
| last-used wave first | 7 624 203 | -46.4% |
| no whole-fighter copies | 7 604 204 | -46.6% |
| one PDA derivation in the settlement | 7 580 585 | -46.7% |
| unchecked indexing in the walking-distance wave | 7 407 533 | -48.0% |

These rows are history: they were measured on earlier benchmark armies, before the 10-unit cap and later catalog changes. On the current armies, `settle_match` (which reads the battle by hand and closes it) totals about 9 891 000 CU with a worst battle of about 879 800 CU.

Tried and dropped, because they cost as much or more: 12 wave slots instead of 8 (-0.1%), three bit-mask versions of the rubble connectivity check (the depth-first fill reaches zone B after touching few tiles and was cheapest), sorting rubble candidates by a hash prefix, caching target tie keys per round, and unchecked indexing on every fighter access (-0.7%, not worth spreading `unsafe` through the engine).
